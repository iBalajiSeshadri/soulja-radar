- Early nominations (first 30) skew to **RB 32% / WR 33% / QB 28%** — the room

- TE barely nominated early (7%); IDP/DEF essentially never (dumped late for $1).

## Auction GAPS / inefficiencies (exploitable edges)
1. **WR is the deepest, most over-supplied position** — 50 WRs drafted/yr, and
   $473/yr goes to WR "depth" (rank 4+). Nominating mid-tier WRs makes rivals
   spend on a position where replacement is cheap. **Best bleed position.**
2. **RB depth similar** — $468/yr on RB rank-4+. Second-best bleed pool.
3. **$1 leftover cash is huge** — ~29 players/yr go for exactly $1 in the last
   30 picks; last-30 avg price is $1.0. Managers routinely leave cash unused then
   dump it at $1. **Edge: deploy your cash on tier-2 value BEFORE the $1 wasteland;
   don't hoard into forced $1 buys.**
3. **TE cliff after TE3** ($18→$12) — if you miss the top-3 TEs, wait; the
   difference between TE4 and a $1 late TE is small. Don't pay up for TE4–6.
4. **IDP/DEF is ~3% of budget** — near-free. Never spend more than $1–3; snipe.
5. **QB after ~QB6 drops fast** ($41→$28) — the SF value is in the QB4–6 band,
   not the top 3 (who go $53–57). Target the "second wave" QBs.

## Nomination strategy implication (answers "deep position to bleed rivals")
Nominate **mid-tier WR and RB** (the deepest pools). Rivals who still need
those slots will spend $30–45 on players whose true replacement is ~$1–5,
draining cap you can then use on scarce QB/elite-RB/TE1-3. This is more robust
than "nominate players you don't like" because it targets *positional depth*,
not your personal preference — so it never suggests a guy you secretly want.
